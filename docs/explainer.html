Transcript PII scrubber

Checks a transcript, or any other text, for the kinds of personal data you choose, and replaces them before the text goes to an AI model, a chat, a ticket, a dataset or a log.

Transcript PII scrubber · Python package hush 0.2.0 · standard library only, no network, no model call

In short

  • On five public PII test sets, with no config, its share preset removed more of the marked personal data than Presidio and GLiNER on four of them, and less on the court cases of TAB. It was tuned on three of the five; the two held out are PII-TRACE (94.3%, against 62.3% and 53.0%) and TAB (68.1%, against 72.6% and 57.5%).
  • A config file says what to look for: which detector packs run, who the known people are, which names to keep, which words are never a name, your own patterns, and which kinds are only flagged for review.
  • Nicknames, initials, names that are also words and towns on their own are its weak spots.
  • Five presets for five jobs. One of them puts the real values back into an AI model's answer.

How it compares

Five openly licensed test sets, each scored the same way for every tool: a marked span counts as removed when none of its letters or digits is left. hush ran its share preset with no config; Presidio ran with its defaults and spaCy's en_core_web_lg; GLiNER is knowledgator/gliner-pii-large-v1.0 with 30 labels at a threshold of 0.3.

  • All five sets are synthetic or legal text, not real call transcripts: no lower-case names, fillers or misheard words. Real transcripts will be harder for every tool.
  • hush was tuned on the Nemotron sets and the chat lines. PII-TRACE and TAB were held out, but their weak categories (PII-TRACE addresses and phone extensions, TAB's years) were known from a first run before the address, phone and date rules were written.
  • PII-TRACE's names are invented words, which suits hush's "not an English word" rule and flatters its name score. The Nemotron sets use realistic names.
  • Presidio ran with its defaults and GLiNER was not tuned either. GLiNER moved a lot with its label list: with 10 plain labels in place of 30 it removed 84.1% on PII-TRACE but 47.3% on Nemotron-PII, and left a name in 1 chat line of 365 where hush left 30.
  • On TAB, Presidio removes more addresses (79.9%) and dates (98.9%); hush removes more application and case numbers (94.0% against 5.6%).
  • bench/public/ has the harness, the dataset sources and the commands.

The agent is on the config's keep list and the customer is a speaker the transcript names, so both stay. Everyone else goes. A removed name with a role word near it is labelled by that role and flagged for a person to review: [family 1] (near “son”), [staff 1] (near “driver”), [staff 2] (near “manager”), [third party 1] (near “neighbour”). The date of birth, account reference and phone go; the order date stays and is flagged, because the config says so.

Three worked configs are in examples/: support calls, a document before an AI model reads it, and school text. The README lists every key.

How it finds a name

In this order. Each step only sees what the steps before it left.

  1. Emails, links and street addresses, whole, so a name inside one never splits it.
  2. Names to keep, in every form: "Priya Shah", "Shah, Priya", "Priya", "Ms Shah".
  3. Names and IDs, found together, the longer winning where two overlap: titled names ("Mrs Pemberton" keeps "Mrs"), your own detectors, logins and handles, the known people in any order and case, names that are also words only in a name's place, the first-name list (22,643 names given to 10 or more babies in England and Wales since 1996), and screenshot misreads.
  4. Identifier rules from the packs: numbers read out as words, phones, postcodes, national numbers, bank details, cards, dates, long numbers, codes, passwords.
  5. A guess at capitalised names from their neighbours, for anyone left. It must not be a run of ordinary words: "Project Alder Review" stays, "Grace Baker" goes.
  6. Every other mention of a name found, in any case: "phiri's" after "Mr Tendai Phiri". A part that is an English word stays in lower case.
  7. A second check, written separately, reads the result and refuses it if an identifier shape is still there.

Its own test sets

Each test set was written by a separate AI agent that never saw the code, with every piece of personal data marked. The general set has 41 texts (12 support calls, four of them lower-case speech-to-text, plus meeting notes, CVs, complaint emails, chat logs, a form, a CSV and more) and 12 texts with no personal data. The school sets are the ones hush 0.1.0 was built on; set 3 was held out from its tuning.

A sighting counts as caught when none of its letters or digits is left in place; a kept title ("Mrs") does not count as a leak. The two inexact restores hold one name in two cases of letter, which comes back in the first case seen. The general set is not fully held out: its first run showed two faults that were then fixed (a kept person lost a nickname to the known list, and "Mr Szymański" was cut at the "ń"). Kept names went from 91.0% to 100% and names overall from 88.2% to 88.5%; the public-benchmark work since moved names to 88.8% and addresses from 57.1% to 78.6%.

The school example: hush 0.1.0 was built for school text, and examples/education/hush.toml keeps or betters its figures. On the held-out school set: 91.0% of pupil names (90.5% in 0.1.0), 97.8% of pupils on the class list, 62.2% of nicknames and initials, 95.9% of adults, 3 of 25 name-free texts touched.

What it misses

  • Nicknames and initials of known people ("Kev", "JK"). Put known nicknames in the known list's Preferred column.
  • Names that are also words, mid-sentence ("Mark", "Hope", "Bill") or in lower case with nothing after them that marks a person ("jade").
  • Lower-case surnames that are also words ("daniel smith") unless the person is known, and names in capitals in forms.
  • Addresses: 79% on the general set. A town on its own, with no street, stays.
  • A year on its own stays unless the config asks for years.
  • Organisation names stay, and a company named like a person may be taken as one. Towns and places that are first names (Paris, Jordan) may be taken too.
  • Anything that identifies without a name: "the only engineer on the night shift". It counts ages, places, jobs, dates and rare details left in a text and warns above a threshold, but removes none of them.

Before using it on real text

  1. Write a config for your text, starting from the closest example, and pass the known people and the names to keep. They are the largest gain.
  2. For another country's names, add them under [names] files, or rebuild the lists from that country's register with tools/build_names.py.
  3. Measure on real text before trusting these numbers: a few hundred real texts, scrubbed, read by someone your data protection lead trusts, and scored with the bench.
  4. Keep the second check on for anything that leaves your own systems.

Call transcripts

transcripts-plan.html sets out the design for call transcripts between an agent and a customer: keep the two of them, remove every other name, flag each by the role the words around it give, and use a language model only to judge the candidates the rules found. Speaker labels, role cues and flags are built; the model pass is not.
