Names in call transcripts

A design for call transcripts between an agent and a customer: keep the two of them, take out every other person's name, and flag each one by the role the words around it give.

Partly built · goes with explainer.html and AGENTS.md in this repository

In short

  • Code finds the names, a model only judges them. Local rules first, a model call second if an approved route exists, a person last for anything unsure.
  • The known list matters most. On the general test set in this repository, names on the known list were caught 99% of the time, against 87% with the first-name list alone in any case. A support team already holds the customer's account and its own agents' names.
  • A model alone misses names. On tutoring dialogues, Gemini 3.1 Pro asked on its own found 77% and 39% of names on two sets; rules proposing candidates with a model ruling on each reached 98% [1].
  • Use a contracted route. For example the paid Gemini API or Vertex AI in London, under Google's data processing terms. Never a free tier.
  • Call it risk reduction, not anonymisation. A scrubbed transcript with its date and team can still point to "the customer whose son took the kettle back" [19].

On the 12 support calls in the general test set (four of them lower-case speech-to-text), the built parts caught 31 of the 34 names that should go with the share preset and the known list, and left every agent and customer name whole.

The pipeline

  1. Keep list from the call record: the agent (from the team's list) and the customer (from the account), with first names, surnames, "Mr X" forms, preferred names, and the speakers the transcript labels by name. These stay.
  2. Local candidates: the account's other named people (exact, and fuzzy up to two letters out), the ONS first-name list in any case, the context rules from hush, the config's own detectors (account and order references), and optionally one Apache-2.0 GLiNER model with a single "person" label at a low threshold [9][10].
  3. Redact the sure ones locally: known-list matches and identifiers (emails, phones, references, card and bank details). Each person gets one stable placeholder across the whole call: [family 1], [staff 2], [person 3].
  4. A model judges the rest. It gets the partly scrubbed text with speaker labels, the keep list, the config's roles and the numbered candidates, in overlapping windows of about 40 turns. It returns JSON only: per candidate a role and a decision (redact, keep, unsure), with the quote and its offsets, plus any names it thinks were missed [21].
  5. Code checks the model. A quote that does not match its offsets is rejected. Nothing outside the redactions may change. A "keep" can never overrule a known-list match.
  6. Flag queue. Every name that is not on the keep list is removed. Names with a role, unsure items, disagreements, fuzzy matches and names that are also words ("Grace", "Will") go to a person before the transcript is stored or shared.
  7. Second check on the output for identifier shapes, as in hush.

The flag rule

A name that is not on the keep list is removed. It is flagged with a role when a cue word the config names sits within four words of it in the same sentence ("my son", "my manager", "my neighbour"), or the model gives it a role. The roles and their cue words are the config's [cues]; examples/support-call/hush.toml has family, staff and third party. A person keeps the first role found for every mention in the call. Names with no role are removed as [person n] and counted.

For school calls, the education example's config has pupil cues ready to turn on ("my son", "in Year 9", "her class", "his homework"), so names near them come out as [pupil n] and flagged.

What the research says

FindingSource
Rules and small models proposing candidates, a local model (Gemma 31B) ruling redact or keep on each: recall 0.98, F1 0.95 to 0.97 on tutoring dialogues. Zero-shot Gemini 3.1 Pro: recall 0.77 and 0.39, worse on long dialogues.[1] 2026
Frontier models caught every mention of a name repeated 6 to 10 times in only 5 to 46% of cases.[5] 2026
Names that are also ordinary words lose 20 to 40% recall in LLMs.[6] FAccT 2026
GPT-4 on 3,505 student forum posts: recall 0.96, precision 0.53, and it fixed grammar it was told to leave.[3] EDM 2024
Presidio as a name finder over-flags: recall 0.83, precision 0.24 on student essays.[2] JEDM 2025
Maths tutoring: Presidio F1 0.38; maths-aware LLM prompting 0.80 to 0.82. Numbers get over-redacted, so domain words and numbers need their own handling.[7] 2026
Few-shot examples help (GPT-3.5 F1 0.53 to 0.83); a per-candidate verifier raises precision, not recall.[4], [2]
Contact-centre tools (AWS, Azure, Google) redact every name or a listed set. None tells a customer's family from the agent's colleagues; the pattern is a per-call allow-list plus a detector.[17] [18]

No published benchmark matches this setting (UK customer calls, transcribed speech). Most of the work above is on education text. Treat these as direction, and measure on your own transcripts.

Gemini: terms and cost

One route, as an example of what to check for any model provider.

RouteWhat Google's pages say (read 24 November 2026)
Gemini API, paidPrompts not used to improve products; covered by Google's processor terms; logged for a limited period for abuse checks, in any country, so no UK residency. UK use must be on the paid tier [11][12].
Gemini API, free or Contributor tierDo not use: the free tier is not for UK use and the Contributor tier trains on your data [11].
Vertex AI, LondonNo training without permission, under the Cloud data processing addendum [22]. Zero retention needs caching and logging turned off and no Search grounding [13]. London offers gemini-3.5-flash and gemini-2.5-flash; newer Flash models are EU-region only [14].

Cost: gemini-3.5-flash on Vertex is $1.65 per million input tokens [15]. A 30-minute call is about 4,500 words, roughly 6,000 tokens; with instructions, examples and overlapping windows, call it 15,000. That is about 2.5 US cents a call, before output.

Google Cloud Sensitive Data Protection's PERSON_NAME detector runs in London and can exclude a list of names per call, but has no independent accuracy figures and cannot tell a customer's son from the agent's manager [16]. A third opinion at most.

Measure before trusting it

  1. Two people annotate 150 to 300 real transcripts inside the approved environment: every name mention, its role (customer, family, staff, third party, other) and its type (full name, first name, nickname, initial, transcription slip, word-name, off the known list).
  2. Report recall per mention and by type, the share of transcripts leaking any name that should go, over-redaction (agent and customer names lost), and cost per call.
  3. If 300 annotated mentions show no misses, the miss rate is under 1% with 95% confidence (the rule of three: 3/n). One miss in 300 does not give that bound.
  4. Keep a third of the set unseen while tuning, and report that third.

Risks and limits

  • Transcription slips spell names in ways no list holds; fuzzy matching and the model help, a person catches the rest.
  • Nicknames and initials stay the weakest case: about six in ten on the test sets in this repository.
  • Context identifies without names. Limit who can read scrubbed transcripts, keep retention short, and keep the account and date apart from the text where you can.
  • Calls can mention children and health. Check with the data protection lead what the contracts allow, and whether the account's other named people may be used as a known list.
  • Gemini prices on the API page change on 1 January 2027 [12].
