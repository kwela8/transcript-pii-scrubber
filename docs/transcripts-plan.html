Pupil names in call transcripts

A plan for customer call transcripts between a support coach and school staff: take out every person's name that is not a known speaker's, and flag the ones that look like pupils.

A design, not yet built · goes with explainer.html and AGENTS.md in this repository

In short

  • Code finds the names, the model only judges them. Local rules first, a Gemini call second, a person last for anything unsure.
  • The school's class list matters most. On the held-out test set in this repository, pupils on it were caught 97% of the time, against 84% with the first-name list alone in any case. The provider already holds each school's roll.
  • A model alone misses names. On tutoring dialogues, Gemini 3.1 Pro asked on its own found 77% and 39% of names on two sets; rules proposing candidates with a model ruling on each reached 98% [1].
  • Use a contracted route. The paid Gemini API or Vertex AI in London, under Google's data processing terms. Never the free tier.
  • Call it risk reduction, not anonymisation. A scrubbed transcript with its school and date can still point to "the Year 9 boy who broke his arm" [19].

The pipeline

  1. Protect list from the call's metadata: the known speakers (the coach and the member of school staff) with their first names, surnames, "Mrs X" forms, initials and likely transcription spellings. These stay.
  2. Local candidates: the customer school's class list (exact, and fuzzy up to two letters out), the ONS first-name list in any case, the context rules from hush, and optionally one Apache-2.0 GLiNER model with a single "person" label at a low threshold [9][10].
  3. Redact the sure ones locally: exact class-list matches and identifiers (UPN, ULN, emails, phones). Each person gets one stable placeholder, [PUPIL_1].
  4. Gemini judges the rest. It gets the partly scrubbed text with speaker labels, the protect list and the numbered candidates, in overlapping windows of about 40 turns. It returns JSON only: per candidate a role (pupil, staff, parent, other) and a decision (redact, keep, unsure), with the quote and its offsets, plus any names it thinks were missed [21].
  5. Code checks the model. A quote that does not match its offsets is rejected. Nothing outside the redactions may change. A "keep" can never overrule a class-list match.
  6. Flag queue. Every name that is not a known speaker's is removed. Unsure items, disagreements, fuzzy matches and names that are also words ("Grace", "Will") go to a person before the transcript is stored or shared.
  7. Second check on the output for identifier shapes, as in hush.

The flag rule

A name is flagged likely pupil when it is not on the protect list and any of these hold: it is on the class list; the model says pupil; or it sits near pupil words ("my son", "in Year 9", "her class", "his homework", "the kids"). Other names (another teacher, a parent, a colleague at the provider) are removed too, labelled by role, but not flagged as pupils.

Presidio as a name finder over-flags: recall 0.83, precision 0.24 on student essays.[2] JEDM 2025
Maths tutoring: Presidio F1 0.38; maths-aware LLM prompting 0.80 to 0.82. Numbers get over-redacted.[7] 2026

Contact-centre tools (AWS, Azure, Google) redact every name or a listed set. None tells pupils from teachers; the pattern is a per-call allow-list plus a detector.[17] [18]

No published benchmark matches this setting (UK school calls, transcribed speech). Treat these as direction, and measure on the provider's own transcripts.

Cost: gemini-3.5-flash on Vertex is $1.65 per million input tokens [15]. A 30-minute call is about 4,500 words, roughly 6,000 tokens; with instructions, examples and overlapping windows, call it 15,000. That is about 2.5 US cents a call, before output, which is small.

Google Cloud Sensitive Data Protection's PERSON_NAME detector runs in London and can exclude a list of names per call, but has no independent accuracy figures and cannot tell a pupil from a teacher [16]. A third opinion at most.

Measure before trusting it

  1. Two people annotate 150 to 300 real transcripts inside the provider's approved environment: every pupil-name mention, its type (full name, first name, nickname, initial, transcription slip, word-name, off the class list).
  2. Report recall per mention and by type, the share of transcripts leaking any pupil name, over-redaction, and cost per call.
  3. If 300 annotated mentions show no misses, the miss rate is under 1% with 95% confidence (the rule of three: 3/n). One miss in 300 does not give that bound.
  4. Keep a third of the set unseen while tuning, and report that third.

Risks and limits

  • Transcription slips spell names in ways no list holds; fuzzy matching and the model help, a person catches the rest.
  • Nicknames and initials stay the weakest case: about six in ten on the test sets in this repository.
  • Context identifies without names. Limit who can read scrubbed transcripts, keep retention short, and keep the school and date apart from the text where you can.
  • Whether the class list may be used for this depends on the school contracts; check with the data protection lead.
